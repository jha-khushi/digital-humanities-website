Khushi Jha’s Digital Humanities Portfolio

Portfolio introduction. This is my work for the Digital Humanities course this semester. I worked with four Indian languages, five Indian books and one Tagore story, mostly using small AI models that run on my own laptop. The four set assignments cover stop words, finding plants and animals with RAG, a synthetic reading of “Subha” and a SKILL file for legal case briefs. I added two of my own: one tests the stop-word lists on real text, the other compares the five books. Open any assignment to see its charts and what I made of the results.

Assignments

Assignment 01

Indic Stop Words: Hindi, Bengali, Gujarati and Tamil

Stop-word lists for four Indic languages in four scripts, grouped by kind of word, with a function that removes them from a sentence.

Key finding: Tamil has far fewer separate postpositions than Hindi, because its grammar is joined to the noun.

Open the assignment page dh-01-indic-stopwords
Assignment 02

Flora and Fauna in Five Indian Books, with RAG

A local RAG pipeline finds the plants and animals named in five Indian books: Kalidasa, the Jataka tales, Bankim’s The Poison Tree, Naidu’s The Golden Threshold and Tagore’s The Gardener.

Key finding: 65 plants and 68 animals. Only 225 of the 392 names the model proposed were real.

Open the assignment page dh-02-rag-flora-fauna
Assignment 03

A Synthetic Reading of Tagore’s “Subha”

A synthetic reading of Tagore’s story about a mute girl: a partial reading, a hypothesis, a local LLM, charts, and an interpretation with Spivak, Rubin and ecofeminism.

Key finding: Nature words stop completely after sentence 98 of 118, when marriage takes Subha to Calcutta.

Open the assignment page dh-03-synthetic-reading-subha
Assignment 04

A SKILL for an LLM: Indian Case-Brief Writer

A SKILL file that makes an LLM write case briefs of Indian constitutional judgments without inventing citations or presenting a dissent as the law.

Key finding: Most of the skill is about what the model must not do.

Open the assignment page dh-04-skill-case-brief
Assignment 05

Testing the Stop-Word Lists on Real Text

Additional, extends Assignment 01. The four stop-word lists tested on the same four Wikipedia articles in each language.

Key finding: The Hindi list removes 41.2% of words; the Tamil list only 14.5%.

Open the assignment page dh-05-stopword-evaluation
Assignment 06

Comparing the Natural Worlds of Five Books

Additional, extends Assignment 02. How often each book names its plants and animals, compared across the five books.

Key finding: The fables are 77% animals, Naidu’s poems 65% plants. Only six names appear in all five books.

Open the assignment page dh-06-comparing-books

Portfolio reflection

Looking back, most of my time did not go into running the models. It went into checking what they gave me. The model suggested 392 plants and animals and only 225 were real; at one point it called a kitchen blade a fish. The stop-word lists looked fine on paper, but on real text the Tamil list removed about a third of what the Hindi one did, which showed me that even a simple tool can suit one language better than another. In “Subha” the charts showed me something I had missed while reading, that nature disappears the moment Subha is taken to Calcutta. Explaining why still needed Spivak and a close reading of the story. That is what I take from this course: the computer is good at showing me where to look, and the reading is still my job.

Optional: methods note

I used Python 3, with NumPy, Matplotlib and NetworkX for the charts, and Ollama to run qwen2.5:7b and nomic-embed-text on my laptop, so no text was sent online. All the literary texts are public domain (Project Gutenberg). I checked the model’s output against the texts and wrote every correction into the code, and I kept the runs that failed.
